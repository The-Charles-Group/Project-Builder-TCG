1) Why Workfront is rejecting the XML

The smoking gun in the rejected exports

In at least one of the XMLs that’s being rejected (ex: ..._c273a9f3.xml), there are dangling predecessor references:

2) Why your durations/end-dates don’t match (even when hours + starts do)

What’s happening

Workfront will either:
	•	trust Start + Duration and recompute Finish, or
	•	trust Start/Finish and recompute Duration, or
	•	roll up summary task dates from children

If your XML has Start/Finish + Duration that don’t agree, Workfront tends to “pick a side” and your Due dates drift.

Your own doc shows the exact off-by-one bug that causes this:
duration_days = max(1, biz_days_inclusive - 1)  ￼
That makes Duration shorter than the Start/Finish span, so Workfront will shift the Finish earlier.

Your doc later shows the correct rule (“DO NOT subtract 1”).  ￼
So the fix is: Duration must be derived from Start/Finish consistently (or vice versa), everywhere, not in only one branch.

Another mismatch source: Step 4 “compression” is shifting by calendar days

In your “compress” logic, you’re shifting task dates using datetime.timedelta(days=shift_days).  ￼
That pushes tasks onto weekends, which then blows up “working duration” expectations and can create Start/Finish/Durations that Workfront reinterprets.

Also: Step 4 is writing timeline_start/timeline_end onto every task using the deliverable window mapping  ￼ — which makes the UI look “right” at deliverable level while the underlying task schedule may not be truly computed the same way Workfront will schedule.

3) Answer to your key question: “What if the app computed the Gantt at task level in Step 4 — would that help?”

Yes. That’s the only way you’ll get stable alignment.

Because in Workfront:
	•	summary task duration/dates are rollups from children
	•	dependencies are interpreted at the task layer
	•	the Gantt you “like to see” structurally is built from leaf tasks

So if Step 4 only computes deliverable spans (and then stretches/labels tasks), you’ll always get mismatches.

4) What the app Gantt will look like after the task-level fix

If Step 4 actually schedules leaf tasks (client_review/general/internal_review/qa) with real Start/Finish dates (Mon–Fri), then:
	•	Deliverables will match Workfront, because deliverables will be min(child_start) → max(child_finish)
	•	The app will no longer “guess” duration only from total hours; it will reflect the same rollup Workfront uses
	•	You should expect:
	•	working duration in the app = working duration in Workfront
	•	If you keep “review windows” (longer spans with low hours), the Gantt will be longer than pure effort-days (and that’s normal)
	•	If instead you make every leaf task duration derive from work-hours only, then the whole schedule will get shorter (and Workfront will match that shorter schedule)

And yes: you’ll still see cases like “3 hours” but “12 days” if you’re treating client review as a window (hours = effort, duration = elapsed working days). That’s not a bug — it’s a modeling choice.
