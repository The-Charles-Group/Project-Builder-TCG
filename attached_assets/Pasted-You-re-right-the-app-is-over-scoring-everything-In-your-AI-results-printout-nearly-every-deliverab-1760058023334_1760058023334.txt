Core logic in the patch

Department intent gating (sticks to your 6 departments).
We classify the RFP text to department intent (e.g., Paid Media + Technology here because of campaign execution/optimization, conversion tracking, GA4/GTM). Deliverables outside the top 1–2 departments are penalized; inside they get a small bonus.
Why: Closes the door on irrelevant departments (e.g., Content/Brand/Big Creative) when the RFP is execution‑heavy.

Execution > Strategy bias.
Names containing buy, activation, trafficking, optimize, onboard, report are boosted; plan, strategy, deck, guidelines are down‑weighted so decks don’t float to the top unless the RFP truly asks for them.
Why: Casa Dragones asks for execution and optimization first, not big strategy decks.

Budget‑aware penalty.
We extract the stated budget (Casa Dragones: “The Budget is $100,000 and cannot go above that.”, slide 5) and down‑weight deliverables whose typical hours (from All_Task_Rows hour columns when present) would likely blow the budget.

Sparsity shaping (the big fix).
After computing base scores, we apply a low‑temperature softmax and then map results into bands with a hard cap on “High” items:

High band ≥ 85%: at most 4 deliverables allowed (tunable).

Mid 70–84% and Low < 70%: everything else is compressed down.
Net effect: You’ll see 2–4 top deliverables, a small mid group, and a long low tail—no more “everything is 90%.”

Self‑check metadata to the UI.
The API returns the detected top departments and budget so humans can judge why the model is choosing what it’s choosing.

Result for this RFP should look like: Paid Media Onboarding / Paid Media Buying & Activation / Paid Media Trafficking & Performance in the High band; Paid Media Reporting and a small dose of Paid Media Planning in Mid; most Creative/Content/Brand work at Low (< 65%)—mirroring what you actually used.

Why the old scores were wrong

The current flow relied heavily on lexical similarity over names, with no gating by department, no budget/timeline awareness, and no calibration—so lots of similarly named items clustered at the top and all looked “very relevant.” You can see that effect across many screens in your printout (90%+ for Creative, Strategy, Content, etc.).
