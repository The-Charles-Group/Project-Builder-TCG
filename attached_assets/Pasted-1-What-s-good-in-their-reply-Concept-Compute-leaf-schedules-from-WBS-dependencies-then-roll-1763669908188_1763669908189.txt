2. Where they’re overcomplicating it (for now)
a) PM-brain vs 8h/day

They frame it as a head-to-head conflict, but for your goal (“match Workfront dates”) there’s a simple answer:

Scheduler that drives XML & app Gantt should use Workfront semantics → 8h = 1 day, FS dependencies, business days.

PM-brain (6.5h × 0.7 focus × resources) can still exist as an advisory / “planning brain” for suggested durations, but not as the thing that sets exported dates.

In other words: they don’t need to reconcile into one formula; they just have different purposes.

b) Manual overrides

They’re thinking about a full constraint system (“override layer applied after scheduler”). That’s nice, but you don’t need all that sophistication to keep current behavior:

Right now, users basically tweak parent dates in your UI.

In Workfront, parent dates are summary, driven by children anyway.

So the simplest safe rule is:

Let the scheduler own all child tasks (L4/L5/L6).
If a user has manually edited a parent (L3/Strategy level), store override_start / override_finish and only override those specific parent dates on export / in your Gantt tooltip.

That means:

Children still line up with Workfront.

Overridden parent dates are mostly cosmetic labels; they don’t break the true critical path.

You can always make overrides “smarter” later.

c) Mixed dependency types

They’re worried about SS + lag, but in your current WBS-based system we just built, your actual exported dependencies are all FS:

L4→L4 and L5→L5 links are straightforward FS.

The SS+lag stuff lives up in the old build_schedule/PM-brain layer, not in the WBS export.

So for the WBS scheduler, it’s fine to:

Implement FS only now, log if you ever detect non-FS, and add SS+lag support later if it actually shows up.
